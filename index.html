What is a scanimation?

A scanimation — also called a barrier-grid animation, kinegram, picket-fence animation, moiré animation or ombro-cinéma — is an optical illusion you can print. The frames of a short animation are cut into thin strips and interleaved into a single image. A transparent sheet printed with black bars and thin slits, the barrier grid, goes on top: as you slide it, the slits reveal one frame at a time and the picture comes to life.

Scanimation Studio is a free online tool to make them: it builds the interlaced image and the matching grid for you, shows a live preview and exports both at their real print size.

How to make a barrier-grid animation

  1. Choose a source: upload the frames as images, load an animated GIF, or type a word and animate it (3D turn, spin, letters appearing). Four to six frames work best.
  2. Tune the grid: vertical, horizontal or circular (rotating) lines, strip width, slit aperture and a continuous or back-and-forth loop.
  3. Set the size in millimetres and the print resolution, and pick a colour style.
  4. Check the preview: the grid slides over the image just as it will on paper.
  5. Export: download the grid (a PNG with transparent slits, for acetate) and the base image, or save the animation to lay several of them out on A4 sheets as two matching PDFs.

Frequently asked questions

Is a kinegram the same as a scanimation?

Yes: kinegram, scanimation, barrier-grid animation, picket-fence animation and ombro-cinéma all name the same effect. KINEGRAM is also a trademark of OVD Kinegram for diffractive security features on banknotes and passports, which are a different technology.

Is Scanimation Studio free?

Yes. It is free, needs no account and runs entirely in your browser: your images are not uploaded.

How many frames should a barrier-grid animation have?

Four to six. Only one frame in N shows through the grid, so more frames make the image darker and harder to read, especially at small sizes.

Which strip width and aperture should I use?

On a home printer, strips of about 0.3–0.4 mm and a slit aperture around 75% give a sharp, readable result. Thinner strips need a more precise printer.

How do I print a scanimation?

Print the image on paper and the grid on transparent acetate, both at 100% ("actual size"), never "fit to page". Lay the acetate over the image and slide it, or pin it at the centre for a circular grid.
