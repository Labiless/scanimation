What is a kinegram? Scanimation and barrier-grid animation explained

A kinegram is a picture that moves on paper. The frames of a short animation are cut into thin strips and woven into a single image; a transparent sheet printed with black bars and thin slits — the barrier grid — goes on top. Slide the grid and the slits reveal one frame after another: the picture comes to life, with no screen and no electricity.

One effect, many names

The same illusion is known by many names, which is why it can be hard to search for:

Name Notes
Kinegram Common in Europe and among designers; also written “kine-gram”.
Scanimation Made popular by Rufus Butler Seder's moving-picture books; often searched as “scanigram”.
Barrier-grid animation The descriptive, technical name: a grid of opaque bars over an interlaced image.
Picket-fence animation The grid looks like a picket fence you look through.
Ombro-cinéma An early 20th-century French toy that scrolled striped pictures behind a slotted screen.
Moiré animation, slit animation Looser names; “moiré” is not strictly accurate, but widely used.

How a barrier-grid animation works

Take an animation of N frames and choose a strip width w:

  1. The base image is built column by column (or row by row): the first strip comes from frame 1, the next from frame 2, and so on up to frame N, then the pattern repeats.
  2. The grid repeats every N × w: one transparent slit about w wide, then black bars covering the other N − 1 strips.
  3. In any position, the slits line up with the strips of one frame only. Move the grid by one strip and the next frame shows; keep moving and the frames play in order.

A few consequences follow directly from this:

  • Fewer frames, brighter picture. Only one strip in N is visible, so 4–6 frames are the sweet spot.
  • Slit aperture is a trade-off. A slit narrower than the strip gives a sharper image; a wider one is brighter but lets neighbouring frames blend in.
  • Scale must be exact. If the grid is printed even slightly larger or smaller than the image, the slits drift out of step and broad bands (moiré) appear.
  • Circular grids use wedges instead of strips: turning the grid around a pin plays the animation, which suits wheels, spirals and orbits.

Kinegram vs. the KINEGRAM security feature

KINEGRAM is also a trademark of OVD Kinegram for diffractive security images on banknotes, passports and ID cards. Those are a different, holographic technology: if you were looking for animated optical illusions to print, you are in the right place.

Kinegram vs. lenticular printing

Both interleave several images in strips. A kinegram uses a sliding grid, so you move the grid; lenticular printing uses a sheet of tiny lenses, so you tilt the print. Kinegrams are cheap and easy to make at home with paper and acetate; lenticular prints are brighter but need special lens sheets.
