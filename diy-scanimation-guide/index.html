How to make a scanimation: a DIY kinegram guide

A scanimation — also called a kinegram or barrier-grid animation — is an animated optical illusion you can print at home. You need a printer, a sheet of paper, a sheet of transparent acetate and about half an hour. This guide shows every step, from choosing the animation to sliding the finished grid.

What you need

  • A printer. A laser printer gives the blackest bars and the sharpest slits; an inkjet works too.
  • Paper or thin card for the base image (matte white is best).
  • Transparent acetate (overhead transparency film) for the grid. Buy the type made for your printer: inkjet film and laser film are different.
  • Scissors or a craft knife and a ruler.
  • Optional: a paper sleeve to slide the grid in, or a brad fastener to pin a circular grid at its centre.
  • Scanimation Studio, the free online tool that builds the image and the grid for you.

Step 1 · Choose the animation

Barrier-grid animations work best with 4 to 6 frames of a short movement that loops: a spinning wheel, a bouncing ball, a galloping horse, a flapping bird, a beating heart. Through the grid you only see one frame in N, so fewer frames mean a brighter, clearer picture.

Bold black-and-white silhouettes read much better than photographs or thin lines. Keep the parts that don't move identical in every frame: they stay solid while the moving part animates.

You can start from three kinds of source:

  • Images: your own frames, drawn or exported from any animation app.
  • An animated GIF: the tool picks evenly spaced frames from it.
  • Text: type a word and animate it with a 3D turn, a spin or letters appearing.

Step 2 · Create the base image and the grid

  1. Open Scanimation Studio and load your frames, GIF or text.
  2. Choose the grid: horizontal or vertical lines that slide, or a circular grid that rotates. The defaults — 0.17 mm strips with a 150% slit aperture (about 0.25 mm) — work well on a home printer.
  3. Set the size in millimetres and the print resolution (300 dpi is a good default).
  4. Watch the live preview, drag the grid by hand, and step through the frames.
  5. Download the grid (a PNG with transparent slits) and the base image. Both carry their print resolution, so they print at the exact size. To print several animations at once, use “Save for printing” and download the two A4 PDFs.

Step 3 · Print at actual size

Print the base image on paper and the grid on acetate. In the print dialog choose 100% or “Actual size” — never “Fit to page”: if the grid is scaled even 1%, its lines drift out of step with the strips and the animation falls apart into bands.

Tip: print a small test first. Measure the printed width with a ruler: it should match the size you set in the tool.

Step 4 · Assemble and slide

  1. Cut out the image and the grid along their edges.
  2. Lay the acetate over the image with the black lines parallel to the strips.
  3. Slide it slowly across the lines: each slit reveals a different frame and the picture moves.
  4. For a circular grid, pierce both pieces exactly at the centre and join them with a brad fastener, then turn the acetate.
  5. For a finished object, glue the image to card and make a paper sleeve that holds the acetate, so it slides straight.

Troubleshooting

Problem Fix
Wide dark and light bands across the image (moiré) The grid was scaled: reprint both at 100% / actual size.
Ghost frames, blurry movement Lower the slit aperture, or use fewer frames.
Picture too dark Use fewer frames or a wider slit aperture.
Black bars look grey or see-through Print the grid on a laser printer, or with the darkest print setting.
Fine details disappear Use bolder shapes, a bigger print size or wider strips.

DIY scanimation project ideas

  • Animated business cards and greeting cards
  • Bookmarks, posters and postcards that move
  • School, STEM and art-class projects about perception and animation
  • Crafts with kids: a galloping horse, a spinning wheel, a bouncing ball
  • Wedding invitations, packaging and gifts with a hidden animation

Questions

Can I make a kinegram without a laser printer?

Yes. Use acetate made for inkjet printers and the darkest print quality. If the bars look too light, have just the grid printed on film at a copy shop.

What size should a DIY scanimation be?

Anything from a business card (about 70 mm) to a poster. Small pieces need bold shapes and few frames; large ones can use more frames and wider strips.

Is the tool really free?

Yes: free, no account, and everything runs in your browser — your images are never uploaded.
